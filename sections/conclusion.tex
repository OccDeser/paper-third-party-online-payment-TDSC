\section{Conclusion}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This work presents a systematic security analysis of third-party payment protocols, revealing a fundamental anti-pattern in their design: the absence of order authentication during user authorization.
This design flaw directly enables order tampering attacks and persists across major payment ecosystems despite their widespread deployment.

We formalize six widely deployed payment protocols from major Chinese payment providers using symbolic models that capture core entities and interaction flows.
Through formal verification with \Tamarin under weakened channel assumptions, we identify a novel variant of order tampering attacks and establish a comprehensive security property framework spanning three dimensions.

To validate our findings, we develop a proof-of-concept attack on Android that successfully tampers with payment orders in real-world environments, affecting both Alipay and PSP-B.
A measurement study of hundreds of popular applications reveals that over 25\% are vulnerable to this attack.
Following responsible disclosure, both payment providers have officially acknowledged the vulnerability.

We further propose a deployable countermeasure that integrates user-side order authentication into the payment flow.
Formal verification demonstrates that this enhanced design satisfies all security properties under weakened channel assumptions, providing a practical path toward more secure third-party payment systems.
