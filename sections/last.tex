\section*{Ethical Considerations}
This study involves the analysis and experimental validation of security vulnerabilities in third-party online payment integrations. 
Particular care was taken to ensure that the research did not cause harm to users, merchants, or payment platforms.

First, all attack experiments were conducted exclusively using accounts fully controlled by the authors. 
No real users were involved, and no real user funds were accessed or affected. 
All transactions were performed with test payments or self-owned accounts, and no financial loss was incurred by users, merchants, or payment platforms.

Second, during the measurement study of Android applications, we only tested whether payment requests could be intercepted or hijacked at the communication level. 
We did not perform actual order tampering attacks, nor did we attempt to manipulate payment amounts or outcomes. 
As a result, application developers and merchants did not suffer any financial or operational damage during the measurement process.

Third, to prevent misuse and reduce the risk of exploitation, we do not disclose the identities or names of affected applications in this paper. 
Vulnerability details are reported in an aggregated manner, and responsible disclosure was conducted with the relevant payment platforms and application developers.

Last, We followed responsible disclosure practices and reported the identified vulnerability to the security teams of WeChat Pay and Alipay prior to publication. 
We also contacted affected application developers with recommended mitigations. 
These steps were taken to ensure that remediation could be initiated before public disclosure.

We believe that the study was conducted in an ethically responsible manner and that the benefits of disclosing the vulnerability and its mitigation outweigh the residual risks.

\section*{Open Science}

To support transparency, reproducibility, and independent validation, we provide research artifacts that enable reviewers and readers to evaluate the technical claims of this paper. 
These artifacts include formal models of six third-party payment protocols and our countermeasure, specifications of the proposed security properties, and scripts used to reproduce the formal verification results.

Due to security and ethical considerations, we do not release proof-of-concept exploit code targeting real merchant applications, nor do we release data that could be used to directly identify vulnerable apps or merchants.
