\section*{Ethical Considerations}
This study analyzes and experimentally validates security vulnerabilities in third-party online payment integrations.
Throughout the research process, strict precautions were taken to ensure that no harm was caused to users, merchants, or payment platforms.

All attack experiments were performed exclusively using accounts fully controlled by the authors.
No real users were involved at any stage, and no real user funds were accessed or affected.
All transactions were conducted either through test payment environments or self-owned accounts, ensuring that no financial loss occurred to users, merchants, or payment platforms.

For the measurement study of Android applications, the analysis was limited to assessing whether payment requests could be intercepted or hijacked at the communication level.
The study did not involve actual order tampering, nor did it attempt to manipulate payment amounts or transaction outcomes.
Consequently, application developers and merchants experienced no financial or operational impact as a result of the measurement process.

To minimize the risk of misuse and potential exploitation, the identities of affected applications are not disclosed in this paper.
Vulnerability findings are reported only in an aggregated manner.
In addition, responsible disclosure was conducted with the relevant payment platforms and application developers.

The identified vulnerabilities were reported to the security teams of WeChat Pay and Alipay prior to publication.
Affected application developers were also contacted and provided with recommended mitigation strategies, allowing remediation efforts to begin before public disclosure.

Overall, the study was conducted in accordance with responsible research and disclosure principles.
We believe that the benefits of systematically analyzing and disclosing these vulnerabilities, together with corresponding mitigations, outweigh the residual risks.

\section*{Open Science}

To support transparency, reproducibility, and independent validation, we provide research artifacts that enable reviewers and readers to evaluate the technical claims of this paper. 
These artifacts include formal models of six third-party payment protocols and our countermeasure, specifications of the proposed security properties, and scripts used to reproduce the formal verification results.

All publicly available artifacts are hosted in an anonymous repository at the following location:
https://anonymous.4open.science/r/third-payment-formal-model-3D9F/
The repository contains our formal models, a detailed README file describing the contents of each artifact, the required software and hardware environment, and step-by-step instructions for reproducing the main results of the paper.

Due to security and ethical considerations, we do not release proof-of-concept exploit code targeting real merchant applications, nor do we release data that could be used to directly identify vulnerable apps or merchants.
