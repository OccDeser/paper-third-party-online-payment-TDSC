\section{Security Properties} \label{sec:security_properties}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------


In this section, we formally model the security properties of third-party online payment protocols.
The primary objective of a third-party online payment protocol is to ensure that 
the merchant, the user, and the service provider reach a consistent view of the transaction status, thereby enabling secure fund transfers.
Specifically, it guarantees the authentication of the payment order and the payment result among the three parties.
The properties discussed in this section all fall under the notion of \emph{injective agreement}~\cite{Lowe1997AuthenticationHierarchy}.

\subsection{Injective Agreement on Order}

The injective agreement on orders is one of the most fundamental properties of third-party online payment protocols.
This property ensures that, during the execution of a third-party online payment protocol, \emph{U}, \emph{PS}, and \emph{MS} are processing the same order.
Once this property is violated, it implies that an attacker is able to tamper the user's order.
As the property involves three parties, we consider the agreement between \emph{PS} and \emph{MS}, and the agreement between \emph{U} and \emph{MS} separately.

\paragraph{\emph{PS} $\leftarrow$ \emph{MS}:} 
From the perspective of \emph{PS}, the injective agreement with \emph{MS} on the payment order can be described as follows. 
If \emph{PS} processes a payment request for some order purportedly from \emph{MS}, 
then \emph{MS} must have previously submitted a payment request for that order.
Moreover, \emph{PS} cannot approve another payment request for the same order. 
This lemma is formalized in first-order logic below:
\begin{flalign*}
  \TAll \; \text{\Tterm{ps}} & \text{\Tterm{ ms order \#i.}} \; \text{\TFact{PSCommit(ps, ms, order)@\#i}} &\\
  \implies & \TEx \; \text{\TLemma{\#j.}} \; \text{\TFact{MSRunning(ms, ps, order)@\#j}} \; \land\\
  \lnot \; &(\;\TEx \; \text{\TLemma{ps2 ms2 \#i2.}} \; \text{\Tterm{\#i2}} \neq \text{\Tterm{\#i}} \; \land\\
  & \hspace{2em} \text{\TFact{PSCommit(ps2, ms2, order)@\#i2}}) 
\end{flalign*}
The facts \emph{Commit} and \emph{Running} were introduced in~\cite{Lowe1997AuthenticationHierarchy}.
A \emph{Commit} fact represents a participant's subjective belief about the local state of its communication peer.
In contrast, a \emph{Running} fact objectively records the actual execution state of the peer in the protocol.
Together, \emph{Commit} and \emph{Running} facts are used to formally specify authentication properties.
In our model, the occurrence of \TFact{PSCommit(ps, ms, order)} indicates that the payment request for \Tterm{order} submitted by \emph{MS} is accepted by \emph{PS}.
The fact \TFact{MSRunning(ms, ps, order)} represents that \emph{MS} has submitted a payment request for \Tterm{order} to \emph{PS}.
The term \Tterm{order} denotes an order that requires user authorization for payment, including information such as the merchant's order identifier, the amount, and a description.

\paragraph{\emph{U} $\leftarrow$ \emph{MS}:} 
From the user's perspective, the injective agreement with \emph{MS} on the payment order is defined as follows. 
If \emph{U} authorizes the payment of an order, then it implies that \emph{MS} must have submitted a request for that order.
Similarly, this lemma is formalized in first-order logic below.
\begin{flalign*}
  \TAll \; \text{\Tterm{u }} & \text{\Tterm{ms goods \#i.}} \; \text{\TFact{UMCommit(u, ms, goods)@\#i}} &\\
  \implies & \TEx \; \text{\TLemma{\#j.}} \; \text{\TFact{MSRunning(ms, u, goods)@\#j}} \; \land\\
  \lnot \; &(\;\TEx \; \text{\TLemma{u2 ms2 \#i2.}} \; \text{\Tterm{\#i2}} \neq \text{\Tterm{\#i}} \; \land\\
  & \hspace{2em} \text{\TFact{UMCommit(u2, ms2, goods)@\#i2}}) 
\end{flalign*}
A merchant's order identifier is a long string of random characters and is very difficult for a user to distinguish.
During the \emph{Payment} phase, a user can typically decide whether to authorize the payment only based on the information of the goods selected during the \emph{Ordering} phase.
Therefore, in this lemma, we use the term \Tterm{goods} to denote the user's order.
Accordingly, the fact \TFact{UMCommit(u, ms, goods)} represents that the user has authorized the payment for the order corresponding to the purchase of \Tterm{goods}.
The fact \TFact{MSRunning(ms, u, goods)} represents that the merchant has initiated a payment request for this order.

\subsection{Injective Agreement on Result}
The payment result is another message that needs to be protected in third-party online payment protocols.
If the agreement on the payment result is violated, 
it means that an attacker can deceive the user or the merchant into believing that a transaction has been completed, 
even though the fund transfer has not actually taken place.
Similarly, we separately study the agreement on the payment result between \emph{MS} and \emph{PS}, and between \emph{U} and \emph{PS}.
  
\paragraph{\emph{MS} $\leftarrow$ \emph{PS}:} 
With respect to agreement on the payment result, from the perspective of \emph{MS}, 
if \emph{MS} receives a notification indicating a successful payment, then \emph{PS} must have sent this notification, 
and moreover, for the same order, \emph{MS} will not receive a second successful payment notification.
The corresponding first-order logic formulation is given below:
\begin{flalign*}
  \TAll \; \text{\Tterm{ms}} & \text{\Tterm{ ps order \#i.}} \; \text{\TFact{MSCommit(ms, ps, order)@\#i}} &\\
  \implies & \TEx \; \text{\TLemma{\#j.}} \; \text{\TFact{PSRunning(ps, ms, order)@\#j}} \; \land\\
  \lnot \; &(\;\TEx \; \text{\TLemma{ms2 ps2 \#i2.}} \; \text{\Tterm{\#i2}} \neq \text{\Tterm{\#i}} \; \land\\
  & \hspace{2em} \text{\TFact{MSCommit(ms2, ps2, order)@\#i2}}) 
\end{flalign*}
In this lemma, the fact \TFact{MSCommit(ms, ps, order)} represents that \emph{MS} has received a successful payment notification for \Tterm{order}.
The fact \TFact{PSRunning(ps, ms, order)} represents that \emph{PS} has sent a successful payment notification for the order \Tterm{order}.
Similar to the agreement on the payment order, the term \Tterm{order} here should also encompass all critical information of the order.

\paragraph{\emph{U} $\leftarrow$ \emph{PS}:} 
From \emph{U}’s perspective, agreement on the payment result means that if \emph{U} observes a successful payment indication on the \emph{PS-App}, 
then \emph{PS} must have sent a corresponding successful payment notification to \emph{U}, 
and moreover, a successful payment notification for a given order will be received at most once.
\begin{flalign*}
  \TAll \; \text{\Tterm{u }} & \text{\Tterm{ps goods \#i.}} \; \text{\TFact{UPCommit(u, ps, goods)@\#i}} &\\
  \implies & \TEx \; \text{\TLemma{\#j.}} \; \text{\TFact{PSRunning(ps, u, goods)@\#j}} \; \land\\
  \lnot \; &(\;\TEx \; \text{\TLemma{u2 ps2 \#i2.}} \; \text{\Tterm{\#i2}} \neq \text{\Tterm{\#i}} \; \land\\
  & \hspace{2em} \text{\TFact{UPCommit(u2, ps2, goods)@\#i2}}) 
\end{flalign*}
In this lemma, the fact \TFact{UPCommit(u, ps, goods)} represents that \emph{U} has received a successful payment notification for the order corresponding to the purchase of \Tterm{goods}.
The fact \TFact{PSRunning(ps, u, goods)} represents that \emph{PS} has sent a successful payment notification for this order to \emph{U} .

\subsection{Injective Agreement of User}

\paragraph{\emph{PS} $\leftarrow$ \emph{U}:} 
The injective agreement of the user is intended to guarantee that each successful payment is authorized by a corresponding password input from \emph{U}, 
and that each password input can authorize payment at most once.
It can be described in first-order logic as follows:
\begin{flalign*}
  \TAll \; \text{\Tterm{u }} & \text{\Tterm{p s \#i.}} \; \text{\TFact{PaymentComplete(u, h(p), s)@\#i}} &\\
  \implies & \TEx \; \text{\TLemma{\#j.}} \; \text{\TFact{UserInputPwd(u, p, s)@\#j}} \; \land\\
  \lnot \; &(\;\TEx \; \text{\TLemma{u2 p2 \#i2.}} \; \text{\Tterm{\#i2}} \neq \text{\Tterm{\#i}} \; \land\\
  & \hspace{2em} \text{\TFact{PaymentComplete(u2, h(p2), s)@\#i2}}) 
\end{flalign*}
In the lemma, the fact \TFact{UserInputPwd(u, p, s)} represents that the user \Tterm{u} inputs the password \Tterm{p} in a session \Tterm{s} to authorize a payment.
The fact \TFact{PaymentComplete(u, h(p), s)} represents that \emph{PS} has received the hash value \Tterm{h(p)} of the password \Tterm{p} sent by \Tterm{u}, and has completed the payment.
