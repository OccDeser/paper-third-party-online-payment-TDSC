\begin{abstract}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Third-party online payment systems, such as Alipay and PSP-B, constitute critical infrastructure for modern e-commerce.
However, their security rests on the unrealistic assumption of fully trusted communication channels. 
While prior studies have identified isolated vulnerabilities, a systematic formal analysis of payment protocol security remains absent. 
This paper presents formal security models for six third-party payment protocols, spanning three major payment scenarios and two dominant payment service providers. 
Our analysis reveals a fundamental design flaw: 
whenever channel integrity is compromised between the merchant client, merchant server, or payment system, order tampering attacks become feasible.
We validate this threat on Android and find that over 20\% of tested merchant applications allow orders tampering through implicit Intent hijacking. 
Following our responsible disclosure, major platforms have deployed patches.
To mitigate this threat, we propose user-side order authentication, where per-user-merchant key pairs cryptographically bind consent to order semantics. 
Formal verification demonstrates its resilience against identified attacks under weak channel assumptions. 
By bridging formal methods with empirical analysis, this work offers actionable guidance for the standardization of secure payment protocols.
\end{abstract}
